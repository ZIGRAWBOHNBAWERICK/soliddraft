# Catallax backend gaps

The backend pins Catallax main revision `27edb84d510d36af92ca1b67419a542a93255afe`. B-rep construction uses `catallax-app`; terminal lattices and network gyroids, constant or graded linearly along a part axis, use `catallax-field::lattice_stage`. Their explicit moments use `catallax_field::mass::properties` on the stage's compiled field, and their mass stand-in uses `catallax_features::stand_in::equivalent_blocks`, `catallax_step::write_stand_in_step` and `catallax_field::mass::surrogate_properties`, checked with the field enclosures' own `contains` and `encloses`, as Catallax's `catallax-stand-in/1` request does. Moments, stand-ins and certified exports run on the caller's work allowance, or on 19,999,999 units when there is none, and a certified export reports the work it used. A STEP write does the same at the 20,000,000-unit application limit. `soliddraft export` holds its whole request, opening and rebuilding included, to that limit and refuses a larger `--max-work`. A larger `mcp --bundle --max-work` does not reach a STEP, field STL or stand-in export, which stops at the limit, and an export inside a caller's larger allowance is refused once the request has used more than the limit. No Catallax code is copied into SolidDraft.

Each solid retains its `IncrementalApplicationBody` and owned `EvaluationCheckpoint`.
For a new solid, the adapter merges operand and read-only context recipes, selects the
longest matching leading checkpoint among them, and calls `catallax_app::build_incremental`.
A limited sweep can reuse its target's geometry without claiming that target's history.
Independent tool recipes are still not assumed to be leading prefixes. The selected
checkpoint is cloned outside the body arena lock, preserving earlier immutable bodies
for undo, branches and concurrent queries. Catallax shares that checkpoint and continues
with persistent geometry, topology and fact storage rather than copying all history.
It validates native step definitions and numerical policy before resuming; a mismatch
restarts evaluation rather than accepting stale geometry.
The adapter emits recipe format 5 from the first solid, so adding an end condition or
an all-faces shell does not discard an otherwise matching checkpoint through a format
upgrade. Formats 3 and 4 stay readable and are never relabelled: a captured question and
an imported STEP body keep format 3 until a reviewed migration.
The document's first-changed-feature cache supplies earlier bodies for parameter edits,
so both model-feature reuse and native prefix reuse apply to the changed suffix.

- Sweep accepts a closed polygon profile and a chain of lines and quarter, half and three-quarter arcs. A fixed-orientation path takes lines only. A rotation-minimising path takes both, with stationary turns at perpendicular joins and at reversals, where the outgoing segment saves its turn axis. Quarter turns need an axis of rational length. A turning profile must lie on one side of the turn axis, in a plane through it. The adapter saves an arc as its centre, axis and turn and derives the exact end, emits native `path` and `sweep` definitions, keeps the profile at its authored placement and retains every segment identity. Faces keep every origin Catallax reports. A one-segment path keeps the face names saved before chains. Other motions and curved profiles show only Coming soon. Loft remains coming soon. This belongs to the batch after 0.2.0.
- Stage 1 profiles are definitions, not sheet bodies. The recipe runner now defers profiles and reports loop count, closure and tags without querying sheet topology, mass or validity. An extrusion or revolution creates and releases the backend's temporary profile handle. Direct sheet queries return `Unsupported`.
- Empty boolean results cannot be expressed by the existing solid/sheet `BodyKind` and mandatory centroid. The adapter returns `Unsupported` without changing the operands. Opening every face by choice is the one empty result the interface carries: `shell_all_faces` returns no body, and the part has none after that feature.
- Measurement proves volume, area, centroid and centroidal unit-density inertia enclosures. The adapter returns their midpoints in the existing floating-point interface. A body with a fitted rolling-envelope blend has no second moments, so it reports no inertia, and Catallax refuses the areas and lengths of faces and edges on the fitted boundary at no work. Those values come from the display mesh.
- Display proves a chord bound and supplies face handles and edge polylines. The adapter forwards the surface angle as Catallax's certified angular deviation, and the edge-length and aspect-ratio requests as Catallax controls. Catallax has no control for the turn along an edge, so a requested curve angle is listed in `Mesh::unavailable_controls`, as is each face where Catallax could not certify a requested control. The adapter reserves part of the chord bound for conversion to the trait's float positions and refuses a request that exceeds that rounding budget. A requested maximum edge length is held to the returned f32 positions: the adapter asks Catallax for the limit less the largest growth f32 rounding can add to a segment, keys the cached mesh by the request as made, and checks every returned triangle edge and edge polyline segment against the requested limit. A limit that rounding growth consumes, or a mesh that still exceeds it, is refused as `Unsupported`; the chord and the limit are never loosened. On-screen display and drawing views use Catallax's unproved preview mesh instead, which claims no bound; exported drawings are drawn from the certified mesh. Where the preview refuses a body, the screen shows the certified mesh. The exit test's hand-made L bracket is one: its flange's edges sit about 1e-14 mm off the plate's, and the preview returns `mesh.precision-or-budget: a boundary constraint was split or overlapped`.
- Topology reads Catallax's proven face areas, face centroids, face and body boxes, edge lengths and half-arc-length points. A plane's normal and origin are enclosed to a double's precision, since `Real::to_f64` is a display midpoint that is wrong in its first digits for a draft's irrational slope. An edge's box is its display polyline grown by the polyline's proven chord error, so it encloses the edge without being tight. `Topology::display_chord_height` is absent.
- Holes, patterns, mirrors, fillets, drafts and extrusions up to a face or the next boundary are stored as native Catallax steps beside the converted `soliddraft-recipe/1` steps, because that format has no spelling for them. Their faces and edges are named by Catallax origins, with a point on the entity only where its origins are shared. The `recipe.json` evidence therefore holds native steps and is not replayable by `soliddraft replay`; scenario views read the saved part instead.
- Fillets cover Catallax's elementary strips: planes meeting planes along lines and planes meeting cylinders along perpendicular rings, including the corners where such strips meet around a block's top and the convex three-plane corners of a triangular prism, whose planes need not be perpendicular, as equal-radius spheres. Straight generator edges between parallel cylinders, or between a cylinder and a plane parallel to its axis, also build. Closed ellipse edges between an oblique plane and a cylinder build, display, measure and export as STEP. Mass properties run on a desktop worker. Measurement still refuses trimmed bands, and STEP still refuses two bands that need disagreeing seam frames on a shared wall. The ring where a narrow cylinder passes cleanly through a wide one at an angle rounds into one fitted band, which displays, selects and writes STEP through `catallax_app::certify_built` and `write_rolling_junction_step`. The adapter reads the band through `JunctionCertificate::saddles` and reports the worst fit of the bands and the walls they trim. Such a body carries the `features.rolling-envelope-fit` mark, so `manifest.exact` is false for it. Volume, area, centroid and the body box answer, and the body patterns and keeps a mirror. Inertia, face measures of the band and the walls it trims, a rail's length and later booleans keep their typed refusals, and STEP refuses a pattern's result, a step that mixes a ring with another strip and a wall with an edge the certificate cannot bound. Shared origin pairs carry a display-polyline near point so picking one generator rounds only that edge. The application also distinguishes shared-face edges when the edge and both support faces retain their recorded fingerprints. Changed, missing or tied candidates still request repair. New shared-generator references persist an optional disambiguation flag; existing references keep their resolution rules. The Catallax recipe is unchanged. Other contacts and unfinished strip ends or junctions retain typed `fillet.*` refusals in diagnostics, with plain explanations in the interface. One such end: an inside fillet along a flange standing in the middle of a plate, with a second inside corner on the flange's far side, returns `fillet.unsupported-overflow`; the same fillet along a flange flush with the plate's edge builds. A plane-to-cylinder fillet crossing countersunk holes builds when the ball rests on the countersink mouth edges, as on the P5 housing, in any exact placement. The adapter builds a hole's centre from its stored local point and the face's exact frame, so that point and a rational frame reach Catallax exactly: the 1e-15 mm rounding of a trip through binary64 world coordinates, taken as exact digits, cost a blend at the mouths ten times the work. Two approximations remain. The command tools project a world point through binary64 before storing it, and a face whose plane or frame axis is irrational keeps the binary64 trip. A radius that clears the mouths gives one analytic torus. So does radius 1, where the rail touches each mouth at one exact point: the flange top splits into five planar pieces that share those contact vertices, and the export keeps them. Its four spline regions are fitted carriers, so a STEP report gives their proven fit in `fit_mm` beside the file's own deviation. A preview of such a body shares the certified display route, so a screen view uses Catallax's default display chord.
- Holes build with flat, drill-point, counterbore and countersink ends at Catallax's standard angles. Two 118-degree blind drill points now answer within the 20M app allowance, as does an 82-degree countersink. Separated parallel countersink cones, drill points and cylinders build beside one another under rigid placement, including the countersink scenario's boss between two holes and its third countersink. Truly meeting non-coaxial cone/cylinder pairs still return `intersect.traced-pair-required`; the scenario checks that refusal preserves the accepted document. A plain hole parallel to a chamfer bevel's axis and strictly inside the bevel's smaller ring also builds, such as the collar scenario's hole in the annulus between the bore and the bevels. A hole whose wall touches or crosses a bevel cone, or whose axis is skew to the bevel's, still returns `intersect.traced-pair-required`. The scenarios cover 90-degree countersinks and a 118-degree drill point; the adapter tests cover the other standard angles.
- The release pin also rounds the crossing-cylinder loop, coaxial circular edges between planes, cylinders, cones, spheres and tori, finite pocket and boss strips, and equal concave three-plane corners. A later fillet rolls over an earlier cylinder strip as a torus, or a sphere strip at equal radii. Corner faces retain `corner.vertex` origins and their sphere sector's material side. The adapter already maps these to `FaceRole::Corner` and `SurfaceKind::Sphere`; it does not read `CornerDefinition` fields or assume a torus carrier or positive contact-circle radius in `Built::fillets`. It passes the kernel's corrected torus axis through display and export. Through-hole mouths round outside the bore; blind floors round inside it. The loop uses the same certificate and fit-report path as a through ring. Inertia and trimmed-band face measures still refuse. `fillet.unsupported-end`, `fillet.unsupported-corner` and `fillet.contact-off-face` have separate user messages. `fillet.radius-too-large` advises a radius strictly below the reported limit, including tangent-shadow equality. Equal-radius crossing poles remain `fillet.unsupported-corner`, with no invented radius limit.

- Drafts cover planar faces and cylinders parallel to the pull, with the neutral plane perpendicular to it for cylinders. Other arrangements return Catallax's unfinished-coverage refusals. The whole body must otherwise fit Catallax's cells, even away from the drafted faces. A hole-free concave planar L bracket drafts on its walls at 3 and 5 degrees. A convex planar body keeps separated through bores along the pull, strictly clear of each other and of every outer wall, or one boss along the pull standing strictly inside a stationary cap, with at most one separate through bore or one through bore strictly inside its footprint, or, alone, one through bore with a countersink at a stationary cap, all made before the draft. A bore or boss touching or crossing an outer wall, the edge of its cap or each other, a blind, stepped or non-axial bore, a bore older than the boss it passes through, a selected bore or boss, several bosses, several bores beside a boss, a bore or boss in a concave base and a fillet strip across the pull still return `draft.unsupported-arrangement`, so drafting the sample bracket still needs some of its features suppressed.
- Extrusions up to a face or the next boundary accept planar limiting faces of a target with no curved walls. Curved limiting faces, and any curved wall on the target even away from the extrusion, return `features.unsupported-limit-surface`. Catallax's curved limits stop a rectangular profile on a target that is a bare stock sphere, cylinder or cone step. SolidDraft's targets are extrusions, revolves and their booleans, so they still take the planar path.
- Proofs over irrational geometry cost far more work. Two walls of a block drafted at 3 or 5 degrees, with a boss on top, with one or two through holes, with a boss made before the draft, with that boss and a through hole beside it or inside it, or with a countersunk through hole, and three walls of a concave L bracket, now build, measure, display and export checked STEP within 20M per request. That covers the draft scenarios only: other angles and arrangements are unmeasured. Proven mass properties of circular copies at 60° also exceeded the default allowance; quarter turns stay rational.
- Chamfers cover Catallax's elementary bevels with one distance: planar strips where planes meet along lines and cones where planes meet cylinders along perpendicular rings. Roll-over, corners where three bevelled edges meet and trimmed circular arcs return Catallax's typed `chamfer.*` refusals. The application has no input for Catallax's unequal setbacks yet.
- Shell uses native Catallax operations. An opening of every face is saved as `[{}]` in the part and sent as `open: [{}]`, never as a list of origins; `[]` stays a closed shell, and a lost picked face stays a reference to repair. Nothing remains of the part after it, so it has no body to export, and later features start again from no body. Convex planar trays support separated parallel cylindrical through bores meeting the same two perpendicular caps. Either cap, both caps or neither can open; thickness edits retain named inner walls and expanded bore walls. Side openings, blind or stepped bores and touching expanded bores remain unsupported. The original planar open-top tray retains its thickness edits and named inner walls. Nonconvex planar skins now build finite rounded inner corners, including an L-shaped open tray. The circular-extrusion fixture retains `distance.cylinder-domain`; arrangements outside the supported offset envelope retain typed distance refusals. Refusals preserve the input body. See [shell coverage and evidence](l08-shell-tool.md).
- Drawing views use polygon hidden-line removal over shared display triangles and Catallax edge polylines. Visibility transitions and curved-face silhouettes remain polygon estimates. Coincident projected rims can fragment into short visible and hidden stretches at mesh boundaries. Complete circular edges retain exact circle metadata across those fragments for dimensions and hole callouts. Polygon mode keeps tangent edges because smooth-edge classification is not converted. Exact silhouettes still return `Unsupported` and need Catallax application queries.
- Plane sections subtract a half-space box through Catallax booleans, map cut entities back through face origins, and hatch ordered display loops of the cut faces. Curved cut boundaries remain polylines. Exact section curves need Catallax conversion queries.
- Whole lines and untrimmed circles convert from Catallax definitions, preserving circle dimensions and circle export after polygon visibility. Trimmed circles, ellipses and intersection curves retain display polylines; exact conversion and general lines-and-arcs approximation remain Catallax work. Face loops use Catallax's default 0.01 mm display chord bound rather than exact curve geometry. Legacy references to trimmed arcs may need reattachment while those edges use polylines.
- Sketch frames within the converter's existing `1e-9` source-axis bound of a cube rotation use an exact integer quaternion. The adapter checks that bound with rational squared errors, preserving the converter's certification contract. Other frames retain the converter's result and refusals. A sweep whose source direction exactly equals the source profile normal or its reverse uses the normal of that same certified rational frame. Independent directions pass through unchanged.
- Rational frame and operation refusals from the stage 1 converter and evaluator remain errors with Catallax's stable code in the message. The adapter does not approximate a refused construction.
- Clean single-body analytic STEP import calls `catallax_import::propose` and `apply`, stores the original bytes, SHA-256 and record, and verifies the saved record with `rebuild` on reopen. The imported fixed recipe receives hash-keyed source bytes through `build_incremental_with_files`. The adapter keeps those bytes for every later feature: its recipe route converts the import as a stand-in, puts Catallax's `import_step` back and evaluates the chain with `evaluate_incremental_with_files`. A lattice stage evaluates the part again without the bytes, so a field on an imported body stays Coming soon. The block and cylinder rollout is a post-0.2.0 addition. A seam repair above the automatic cap saves `accept:<tolerance-mm>:<approver>` under the exact `Proposal.question`. Its `face_tolerances` hold each repaired face's attained deviation, apart from the approved limit, the vertex movement and any export's output error. The record stays with the import feature and does not certify a face a later feature changes. The whole-face proof covers straight, strictly convex planar polygons with one boundary. Spline files, general cone angles, sphere and torus poles, curved, nonconvex and holed repairs and IGES import remain outside this coverage. Multiple solids are refused rather than merged. Existing repair behavior is unchanged. A part saved with a `catallax-import-repair/1` record still opens: its import is redone and certified again, the result must match what the record says, and the next save writes `/2`. The record is never relabelled. Self-intersecting solids are refused, and an undecided self-intersection check remains a refusal with the two faces and reason.

The desktop, browser, command-line and server paths use Catallax. SMLib, its C++ bridge and submodule, its golden corpus and its separate WebAssembly module have been removed.

The Catallax acceptance tests repeat successful box, cylinder, union and subtract recipes and check failure preservation. Shell tests cover the planar tray's material volume, inward-facing walls and STEP proof, together with exact unsupported-domain refusals and input preservation. The dedicated rendered shell test exercises the editor, face picking, edit, persistence and application export. The application scenarios build and edit each switched-on feature. Profile steps report deferred-profile facts; every solid-producing step retains the full topology, mass, validity and digest summary.

The interface fragments since the previous pin also change saddle display sampling and measurement enclosures. The adapter consumes the returned mesh positions and indices without assuming evenly spaced saddle columns or a power-of-two span count. Field preview OBJ headers are irrelevant here because SolidDraft does not save a preview OBJ. Curved entity-pair readout is an opt-in API; the existing measurement routes keep their typed refusals. These changes require no recipe migration.

A 40 x 30 x 12 mm block with all four uprights rounded at R2 and its top loop then rounded at R1 builds and displays, but STEP exceeds the application's 20M work cap at this pin. A top round over one rounded upright exports at both smaller and equal radii. The larger case retains `numbers.operation-budget` and a calculation-limit message; no export allowance is raised.

A full symmetric union of equal crossing cylinders can stop at an ambiguous application edge reference before the fillet evaluator runs. The app retains `recipe.ambiguous-reference` for that failure and leaves the part unchanged. Starting the narrow cylinder on the crossing-axis plane instead reaches `mesh.precision-or-budget` while reading topology at this pin. These arrangements remain outside the completed app acceptance; neither is evidence that singular fillet geometry builds.
