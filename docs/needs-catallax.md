# Catallax backend gaps

The [adapter manifest](../crates/soliddraft-kernel-catallax/Cargo.toml) records the Catallax pin. The pinned backend supports manufacturing information in STEP export and import, feature recognition that reads opposed coaxial holes as separate holes, exact drawing views and clash, clear or unresolved body relations. It also supports exact face-relative extrusions, ellipse profiles, fillets, unions of provably disjoint solids and stable construction names. B-rep construction uses `catallax-app`; terminal lattices and network gyroids use `catallax-field::lattice_stage`. Their explicit moments use `catallax_field::mass::properties` on the stage's compiled field, and their mass stand-in uses `catallax_features::stand_in::equivalent_blocks`, `catallax_step::write_stand_in_step` and `catallax_field::mass::surrogate_properties`, checked with the field enclosures' own `contains` and `encloses`, as Catallax's `catallax-stand-in/1` request does. Moments, stand-ins and certified exports run on the caller's work allowance, or on the 100,000,000-unit application limit when there is none, and a certified export reports the work it used. A STEP write does the same. `soliddraft export` holds its whole request, opening and rebuilding included, to that limit and refuses a larger `--max-work`. A larger `mcp --bundle --max-work` does not reach a STEP, field STL or stand-in export, which stops at the limit, and an export inside a caller's larger allowance is refused once the request has used more than the limit. No Catallax code is copied into SolidDraft.

Face-relative tools can be copied by feature patterns and used in planar first-hit targets. Field compilation resolves their supports through certified planar-boundary recovery; carriers outside that path refuse rather than reuse removed faces.

Catallax `801cb656751d463237c116437c78a03cd873e434` adds certified STEP import for rectangular trims on equal-weight bilinear surfaces. SolidDraft's lattice and gyroid editors offer Constant, Linear along an axis, Distance from faces (whole skin or selected faces), Radial from an axis, and From a point. Catallax also supports oblique linear grades in its field recipes. SolidDraft still needs an input for the line through the oblique grade's anchors. Catallax `e3ef8f8fce7c20acf1d6dd5a2d19dbd9d7640b45` added file-aware lattice and gyroid stages and the shared periodic spline display mesher. The current pin is Catallax main `6e4b5f89e1396d314e991b24fd777bac037526dd`. It is 0.5.0 plus a STEP writer that adds a finish's graphical callout and the AP242 validation properties ([Catallax changes](https://github.com/BohnBawerick/catallax/pull/764)). Catallax `ccae79ab5b39f5b2adbbd0d6827a4f9b3ed0bfc3` added extrusions that start on a trimmed face, planar or curved, and limits on curved faces, in recipe format 6 ([Catallax changes](https://github.com/BohnBawerick/catallax/pull/756)). SolidDraft still uses constant thickness for shells. The spherical scan fitter has no SolidDraft command. [Catallax changes](https://github.com/BohnBawerick/catallax/pull/750).

Each solid retains its `IncrementalApplicationBody` and owned `EvaluationCheckpoint`.
For a new solid, the adapter merges operand and read-only context recipes, selects the
longest matching leading checkpoint among them, and calls `catallax_app::build_incremental`.
A limited sweep can reuse its target's geometry without claiming that target's history.
Independent tool recipes are still not assumed to be leading prefixes. The selected
checkpoint is cloned outside the body arena lock, preserving earlier immutable bodies
for undo, branches and concurrent queries. Catallax shares that checkpoint and continues
with persistent geometry, topology and fact storage rather than copying all history.
It validates native step definitions and numerical policy before resuming; a mismatch
restarts evaluation rather than accepting stale geometry.
The adapter emits recipe format 6 from the first solid, so adding an end condition or
an all-faces shell does not discard an otherwise matching checkpoint through a format
upgrade. Formats 3 and 4 stay readable and are never relabelled: a captured question and
an imported STEP body keep format 3 until a reviewed migration.
The document's first-changed-feature cache supplies earlier bodies for parameter edits,
so both model-feature reuse and native prefix reuse apply to the changed suffix.

- Sweep accepts a closed polygon profile and a chain of lines and quarter, half and three-quarter arcs. A fixed-orientation path takes lines or one rational spline. Spline control points, positive weights and clamped knots persist through editing and reopening. Catallax refuses returning or crossing spline paths when it cannot prove the sweep is safe. A rotation-minimising path takes both, with stationary turns at perpendicular joins and at reversals, where the outgoing segment saves its turn axis. Quarter turns need an axis of rational length. A turning profile must lie on one side of the turn axis, in a plane through it. The adapter saves an arc as its centre, axis and turn and derives the exact end, emits native `path` and `sweep` definitions, keeps the profile at its authored placement and retains every segment identity. Faces keep every origin Catallax reports. A one-segment path keeps the face names saved before chains. Other motions and curved profiles show only Coming soon.
- Profiles are definitions, not sheet bodies. The recipe runner now defers profiles and reports loop count, closure and tags without querying sheet topology, mass or validity. An extrusion or revolution creates and releases the backend's temporary profile handle. Direct sheet queries return `Unsupported`.
- Empty boolean results cannot be expressed by the existing solid/sheet `BodyKind` and mandatory centroid. The adapter returns `Unsupported` without changing the operands. Opening every face by choice is the one empty result the interface carries: `shell_all_faces` returns no body, and the part has none after that feature.
- Measurement proves volume, area, centroid and centroidal unit-density inertia enclosures. The adapter returns their midpoints in the existing floating-point interface. A body with a fitted rolling-envelope blend has no second moments, so it reports no inertia, and Catallax refuses the areas and lengths of faces and edges on the fitted boundary at no work. Those values come from the display mesh.
- A body Catallax builds within a stated tolerance comes back as `EvaluatedApplicationOutcome::Numerical`. The adapter builds through `catallax_app::build_incremental_with_files_without_display` and retains either certified or numerical outcomes. Rolling-ball fillets try exact construction first, then roll within 1e-7 mm using the whole request allowance, including a pocket rim that runs from a flat face onto a rounded one. A refusal retains the exact construction's code and the general construction's reason; a proved radius limit is reported as `fillet.radius-too-large`. The interface shows the general reason unless that construction could not take the input body. Numerical bodies are never cast to exact ones. For display and measurement, the adapter writes numerical STEP through `catallax_step::numerical::build`, previews it through `catallax_mesh::preview_step` in `sample_through_step`, and names faces and edges by the writer's definition keys. Picking, face areas, edge lengths and mass read that unproved sample. Approximate surface display and samples that omit a face are refused. Mass integration uses the sample's box centre and requires a closed, consistently wound mesh. Measurements carry `Certainty::Approximate` and claim no bound. Constructed numerical bodies report Catallax's stated tolerance in the tree and Details. STEP export records that tolerance and length rounding separately in `StepReport::approximate`; `max_deviation` is "not established" and `within_tolerance` is false. Display and export inherit the caller's allowance or the app's ordinary request allowance, not the writer's smaller standalone guard. Import STEP keeps source-certified results first and otherwise uses default tolerant admission; numerical imports are Tolerant, without a whole-body certificate. The app labels numerical exports approximate; `soliddraft export` refuses a checked tolerance it cannot confirm. Features and queries requiring an exact input, support or context remain Coming soon rather than replaying numerical geometry as exact. A through hole in a numerical build is the exception: Catallax's one model (3.0B) drills it by its construction for bodies with tolerant entities, square to the planar faces it crosses. The adapter takes the hole's frame from the planar face's exact support, rebuilds the recipe, keeps the input's face names by their definition keys, and refuses a rebuild that comes back exact. A blind hole, a counterbore or a countersink there is refused by Catallax (`hole.numerical-tool`). Another kernel rebuilds a numerical body's recipe rather than taking an exact-model handover. The `numerical-stand-in` feature supplies a deterministic numerical extrusion for tests independently of the rolling-ball producer.
- Display proves a chord bound and supplies face handles and edge polylines. The adapter forwards the surface angle as Catallax's certified angular deviation, and the edge-length and aspect-ratio requests as Catallax controls. Catallax has no control for the turn along an edge, so a requested curve angle is listed in `Mesh::unavailable_controls`, as is each face where Catallax could not certify a requested control. The adapter reserves part of the chord bound for conversion to the trait's float positions and refuses a request that exceeds that rounding budget. A requested maximum edge length is held to the returned f32 positions: the adapter asks Catallax for the limit less the largest growth f32 rounding can add to a segment, keys the cached mesh by the request as made, and checks every returned triangle edge and edge polyline segment against the requested limit. A limit that rounding growth consumes, or a mesh that still exceeds it, is refused as `Unsupported`; the chord and the limit are never loosened. On-screen display and drawing previews use Catallax's unproved preview mesh. Exports use Catallax drawing queries. Where the preview refuses a body, the screen shows the certified mesh. Only field-only features, lattices and gyroids, draw from a sample of the distance field. Every other body, its early preview included, draws from the boundary model. The adapter test `only_field_only_features_preview_from_the_field` holds that rule. The display-quality regression keeps such a case by mounting an unconstrained flange through a separate reference plane. Direct face attachment previews that flange without the fallback.
- Topology reads Catallax's proven face areas, face centroids, face and body boxes, edge lengths and half-arc-length points. A plane's normal and origin are enclosed to a double's precision, since `Real::to_f64` is a display midpoint that is wrong in its first digits for a draft's irrational slope. An edge's box is its display polyline grown by the polyline's proven chord error, so it encloses the edge without being tight. `Topology::display_chord_height` is absent.
- Holes, patterns, mirrors, fillets, drafts and extrusions up to a face or the next boundary are stored as native Catallax steps beside the converted `soliddraft-recipe/1` steps, because that format has no spelling for them. Their faces and edges are named by Catallax origins, with a point on the entity only where its origins are shared. The `recipe.json` evidence therefore holds native steps and is not replayable by `soliddraft replay`; scenario views read the saved part instead.
- Fillets cover Catallax's elementary strips: planes meeting planes along lines and planes meeting cylinders along perpendicular rings, including the corners where such strips meet around a block's top and the convex three-plane corners of a triangular prism, whose planes need not be perpendicular, as equal-radius spheres. Straight generator edges between parallel cylinders, or between a cylinder and a plane parallel to its axis, also build. Closed ellipse edges between an oblique plane and a cylinder build, display, measure and export as STEP. Mass properties run on a desktop worker. Measurement still refuses trimmed bands, and STEP still refuses two bands that need disagreeing seam frames on a shared wall. The ring where a narrow cylinder passes cleanly through a wide one at an angle rounds into one fitted band, which displays, selects and writes STEP through `catallax_app::certify_built` and `write_rolling_junction_step`. The adapter reads the band through `JunctionCertificate::saddles` and reports the worst fit of the bands and the walls they trim. Such a body carries the `features.rolling-envelope-fit` mark, so `manifest.exact` is false for it. Volume, area, centroid and the body box answer, and the body patterns and keeps a mirror. Inertia, face measures of the band and the walls it trims, a rail's length and later booleans keep their typed refusals, and STEP refuses a pattern's result, a step that mixes a ring with another strip and a wall with an edge the certificate cannot bound. Shared origin pairs carry a display-polyline near point so picking one generator rounds only that edge. The application also distinguishes shared-face edges when the edge and both support faces retain their recorded fingerprints. Changed, missing or tied candidates still request repair. New shared-generator references persist an optional disambiguation flag; existing references keep their resolution rules. The Catallax recipe is unchanged. Other contacts and unfinished strip ends or junctions retain typed `fillet.*` refusals in diagnostics, with plain explanations in the interface. One such end: an inside fillet along a flange standing in the middle of a plate, with a second inside corner on the flange's far side, returns `fillet.unsupported-overflow`; the same fillet along a flange flush with the plate's edge builds. A plane-to-cylinder fillet crossing countersunk holes builds when the ball rests on the countersink mouth edges, as on the P5 housing, in any exact placement. The adapter builds a hole's centre from its stored local point and the face's exact frame, so that point and a rational frame reach Catallax exactly: the 1e-15 mm rounding of a trip through binary64 world coordinates, taken as exact digits, cost a blend at the mouths ten times the work. Two approximations remain. The command tools project a world point through binary64 before storing it, and a face whose plane or frame axis is irrational keeps the binary64 trip. A radius that clears the mouths gives one analytic torus. So does radius 1, where the rail touches each mouth at one exact point: the flange top splits into five planar pieces that share those contact vertices, and the export keeps them. Its four spline regions are fitted carriers, so a STEP report gives their proven fit in `fit_mm` beside the file's own deviation. A preview of such a body shares the certified display route, so a screen view uses Catallax's default display chord.
- A fillet carries only its edges and its radius. Catallax's recipe has an overflow policy and a corner choice for fillets in section 4.5.1 (PRs 318 and 332). SolidDraft asks the user for neither. The adapter leaves both fields absent, so Catallax builds its defaults: the fillet rolls over neighbouring faces and closes a partly filleted corner as a cliff. SolidDraft does not offer Catallax's variable-radius fillets (section 4.5.2).
- Since [Catallax PR 331](https://github.com/BohnBawerick/catallax/pull/331), a straight roll-over run whose foot reaches a whole convex boundary edge before the requested radius builds at that smaller radius, and Catallax keeps the request in `requested_radius`. SolidDraft never builds a radius other than the one the feature records. The adapter refuses such a fillet with `fillet.radius-too-large` and the radius limit, as Catallax did at v0.1.4, so the panel says "Try a radius under 10 mm" for R50 on a 10 mm high block. A radius that consumes the face exactly, such as R10 on that block, builds. The `fillet-face-limit` scenario checks both.
- Holes build with flat, drill-point, counterbore and countersink ends at Catallax's standard angles. Two 118-degree blind drill points now answer within the 20M app allowance, as does an 82-degree countersink. Separated parallel countersink cones, drill points and cylinders build beside one another under rigid placement, including the countersink scenario's boss between two holes and its third countersink. Truly meeting non-coaxial cone/cylinder pairs still return `intersect.traced-pair-required`; the scenario checks that refusal preserves the accepted document. A plain hole parallel to a chamfer bevel's axis and strictly inside the bevel's smaller ring also builds, such as the collar scenario's hole in the annulus between the bore and the bevels. A hole whose wall touches or crosses a bevel cone, or whose axis is skew to the bevel's, still returns `intersect.traced-pair-required`. The scenarios cover 90-degree countersinks and a 118-degree drill point; the adapter tests cover the other standard angles.
- The release pin also rounds the crossing-cylinder loop, coaxial circular edges between planes, cylinders, cones, spheres and tori, finite pocket and boss strips, and equal concave three-plane corners. A later fillet rolls over an earlier cylinder strip as a torus, or a sphere strip at equal radii. Corner faces retain `corner.vertex` origins and their sphere sector's material side. The adapter already maps these to `FaceRole::Corner` and `SurfaceKind::Sphere`; it does not read `CornerDefinition` fields or assume a torus carrier or positive contact-circle radius in `Built::fillets`. It passes the kernel's corrected torus axis through display and export. Through-hole mouths round outside the bore; blind floors round inside it. The loop uses the same certificate and fit-report path as a through ring. Inertia and trimmed-band face measures still refuse. `fillet.unsupported-end`, `fillet.unsupported-corner` and `fillet.contact-off-face` have separate user messages. `fillet.radius-too-large` advises a radius strictly below the reported limit, including tangent-shadow equality. Equal-radius crossing poles remain `fillet.unsupported-corner`, with no invented radius limit.
- All 48 sample-part edges fillet individually at R1. The two cylinder/plane branches with shared end faces retain their existing curve classification and use unchanged edge and support fingerprints to distinguish their names. Changed geometry asks for another pick rather than silently switching branches.

- Drafts cover planar faces and cylinders parallel to the pull, with the neutral plane perpendicular to it for cylinders. Other arrangements return Catallax's unfinished-coverage refusals. The whole body must otherwise fit Catallax's cells, even away from the drafted faces. A hole-free concave planar L bracket drafts on its walls at 3 and 5 degrees. A convex planar body keeps separated through bores along the pull, strictly clear of each other and of every outer wall, or one boss along the pull standing strictly inside a stationary cap, with at most one separate through bore or one through bore strictly inside its footprint, or, alone, one through bore with a countersink at a stationary cap, all made before the draft. A bore or boss touching or crossing an outer wall, the edge of its cap or each other, a blind, stepped or non-axial bore, a bore older than the boss it passes through, a selected bore or boss, several bosses, several bores beside a boss, a bore or boss in a concave base and a fillet strip across the pull still return `draft.unsupported-arrangement`, so drafting the sample bracket still needs some of its features suppressed.
- SolidDraft writes recipe format 6. Extrusions up to a face or the next boundary stop on planar and Bézier spline faces of any target, and on the round face of a target whose last step extrudes one circle. Catallax proves cylinder, cone, sphere and torus limits only on stock primitive steps, which SolidDraft does not author, so revolved faces and cylinders with later features refuse. A blind start on a Bézier spline face builds, but the union or cut of that solid with the spline body returns `intersect.bezier-plane-oblique`, because Bézier extrusion sections need a plane normal to the generators. Extrude From a curved face uses `extrude_from`: a blind sweep from the first crossing of the face to the face moved along the direction, with Direction 2 as a negative distance from the same crossing. Up to next and Up to face from a curved start, torus starts other than coaxial annuli, and spline starts beyond rectangular profiles in the chord and generator chart still need Catallax work; they are refused with `features.unsupported-curved-limit`, shown in plain words.
- Proofs over irrational geometry cost far more work. Two walls of a block drafted at 3 or 5 degrees, with a boss on top, with one or two through holes, with a boss made before the draft, with that boss and a through hole beside it or inside it, or with a countersunk through hole, and three walls of a concave L bracket, now build, measure, display and export checked STEP within 20M per request. That covers the draft scenarios only: other angles and arrangements are unmeasured. Proven mass properties of circular copies at 60° also exceeded the default allowance; quarter turns stay rational.
- Chamfers cover Catallax's elementary bevels with one distance or two unequal setbacks: planar strips where planes meet along lines and cones where planes meet cylinders along perpendicular rings. Roll-over, corners where three bevelled edges meet and trimmed circular arcs return Catallax's typed `chamfer.*` refusals.
- Shell uses native Catallax operations. An opening of every face is saved as `[{}]` in the part and sent as `open: [{}]`, never as a list of origins; `[]` stays a closed shell, and a lost picked face stays a reference to repair. Nothing remains of the part after it, so it has no body to export, and later features start again from no body. Convex planar trays support separated parallel cylindrical through bores meeting the same two perpendicular caps. Either cap, both caps or neither can open; thickness edits retain named inner walls and expanded bore walls. Side openings, blind or stepped bores and touching expanded bores remain unsupported. The original planar open-top tray retains its thickness edits and named inner walls. Nonconvex planar skins now build finite rounded inner corners, including an L-shaped open tray. The R2 x 5 circular-extrusion fixture now builds a closed shell at wall 1 through [Catallax's exact sewn analytic-shell proof](https://github.com/BohnBawerick/catallax/pull/496); arrangements outside the supported offset envelope retain typed distance refusals. Refusals preserve the input body. See [Shell](modelling.md#shell).
- Drawing creation and rebuild use Catallax's hidden-line views (`catallax_app::views::hidden_line_view`), each stroke drawn by `stroke_drawing` as a line, arc, circle or NURBS curve. Silhouettes come from `contour_generators`, plane sections from `section_curves` cut face by face, edge curves from `edge_drawing` and face loops from `face_drawing_loops`, all through `drawing_curve`. Tangent edges are left out unless the view shows them, seams unless it asks for them. An edge in a section plane is tagged with its face that leaves the plane. A fitted curve (an intersection branch, or a torus outline seen obliquely) is marked approximate on its own; a view whose cuts or visibility Catallax measured rather than proved (`app.view-split`, `app.view-ray`, `app.view-short-piece`) carries those codes and every curve of it is approximate (decision Q14). A refusal, including `app.view-curve` for parabola and hyperbola edges and an exhausted allowance, reaches the drawing as an error and is never replaced by a mesh view. Bodies without an exact drawing solid can use mesh previews. Exact drawing queries for these bodies refuse with `app.view-solid`. Section hatch boundaries inherit fitting marks from the cap face loops. General lines-and-arcs approximation remains Catallax work.
- Sketch frames within the converter's existing `1e-9` source-axis bound of a cube rotation use an exact integer quaternion. The adapter checks that bound with rational squared errors, preserving the converter's certification contract. Other frames retain the converter's result and refusals. A sweep whose source direction exactly equals the source profile normal or its reverse uses the normal of that same certified rational frame. Independent directions pass through unchanged.
- Rational frame and operation refusals from the recipe converter and evaluator remain errors with Catallax's stable code in the message. The adapter does not approximate a refused construction.
- Default STEP import first tries source certification through `propose_source` and `apply_source`, including their repair questions and approvals. If that path cannot build a body, it uses `catallax_import::tolerant::opened::open_step` with the default controls. Source-certified results keep the higher Certified label; numerical and locally fitted bodies are Tolerant, without a whole-body deviation bound. A checked model of healed geometry does not certify the original source: both the body outcome and actual storage determine authority. Only an unchanged, non-approximate valid solid with checked storage may be normalized through the certified STEP writer into an exact feature checkpoint. Other checked healed models are displayed and exported from their actual geometry while retaining tolerant authority. Cold admission uses the ordinary app allowance, and resource-incomplete results are not cached as final. Each admitted body is independent of refused siblings, and a body whose display fails is not published. Original bytes, digest, controls, body tiers and native evidence are saved and reverified on reopen. Saved certified repairs and IGES imports retain explicit approvals, face tolerances and source-incidence certificates through `apply_source` and `rebuild_source`. Approved source repairs save `accept:<tolerance-mm>:<approver>` under the original question. Attained face deviation, approved movement and export error remain separate quantities; an import record does not certify a later feature's changed face. Older repair records are reverified, not relabelled. Certified checkpoints retain their hash-keyed source bytes for later features. Lattice and Gyroid pass those bytes to the native stage for preview and certified print export. A print at a different accuracy rebuilds the stage with the same source bytes and minimum detail. Admission and display refusals are reported in plain words.
  Source-certified surface models retain the existing sheet reader. Faceted B-reps are not refused solely by their representation name; each body retains its actual certified or tolerant authority.

The desktop, browser, command-line and server paths use Catallax. SMLib, its C++ bridge and submodule, its golden corpus and its separate WebAssembly module have been removed.

The Catallax acceptance tests repeat successful box, cylinder, union and subtract recipes and check failure preservation. Shell tests cover the planar tray's material volume, inward-facing walls and STEP proof, together with exact unsupported-domain refusals and input preservation. The dedicated rendered shell test exercises the editor, face picking, edit, persistence and application export. The application scenarios build and edit each switched-on feature. Profile steps report deferred-profile facts; every solid-producing step retains the full topology, mass, validity and digest summary.

The interface fragments since the previous pin also change saddle display sampling and measurement enclosures. The adapter consumes the returned mesh positions and indices without assuming evenly spaced saddle columns or a power-of-two span count. Field preview OBJ headers are irrelevant here because SolidDraft does not save a preview OBJ. Curved entity-pair readout is an opt-in API; the existing measurement routes keep their typed refusals. These changes require no recipe migration.

A 40 x 30 x 12 mm block with all four uprights rounded at R2 and its top loop then rounded at R1 builds and displays. At the former pin its STEP export exceeded the application's former 20M work cap and refused with `numbers.operation-budget` and a calculation-limit message. At this pin the app's own STEP export writes it as one solid within tolerance on 431,349 work units, under the 20M target (`upright_and_top_loop_rounds_export_or_refuse_on_one_application_allowance` in `release_geometry.rs`, run with tests at opt-level 2 on Linux). A top round over one rounded upright exports at both smaller and equal radii.

The reverse order builds since [Catallax PR 340](https://github.com/BohnBawerick/catallax/pull/340): R1 rounds on the four uprights of that block after its top loop is rounded at R2. The build takes about 2 s, the screen shows it through Catallax's unproved preview mesh and it exports as one STEP solid within tolerance. Its certified mesh exceeds the application's work allowance with `application.work-limit`, and its exact volume took about five minutes on a desktop.

A full symmetric union of equal crossing cylinders can stop at an ambiguous application edge reference before the fillet evaluator runs. The app retains `recipe.ambiguous-reference` for that failure and leaves the part unchanged. Starting the narrow cylinder on the crossing-axis plane instead reaches `mesh.precision-or-budget` while reading topology at this pin. These arrangements remain outside the completed app acceptance; neither is evidence that singular fillet geometry builds.

## Numerical STEP reopening

The owner pocket-rim R2 fillet's STEP reopens as a tolerant body on the ordinary 100,000,000-unit opening allowance. The import keeps all 19 faces and the bounding box within 0.000001 mm. SolidDraft shows the approximate display and refuses to estimate mass until the display closes. Closing that display is a follow-on. [Fillet acceptance](../crates/soliddraft-app/tests/fillet_acceptance.rs).

An import that exhausts the allowance says “This file is too complex to open yet” and leaves the existing part intact.
